# Nigeria Ride-Hailing Market Intelligence Report

## Project Title
Nigeria Ride-Hailing Market Intelligence Report – Lagos & National Context  
(Uber • Bolt • inDrive • LagRide)

## Project Objectives
- To provide a comprehensive market intelligence view of the Nigerian ride-hailing industry following Uber’s exit on 2 September 2026.
- To analyse market size, growth projections, competitive positioning, pricing structures, and driver/rider economics.
- To identify key pain points, safety and trust gaps, and structural opportunities for existing players and potential new entrants.
- To synthesise secondary public data into clear, actionable insights that support strategic decision-making.

## Dataset Used
Dataset: Secondary public sources only (as of early September 2026)  
Sources include Statista, TechCabal, Technext24, Mordor Intelligence, Bolt/Ipsos reports, news outlets (BusinessDay, TheCable, Punch, etc.), app store reviews, and public price trackers.  
All major figures are clearly labelled as **Sourced**, **Estimated**, or **Assumption**.

## Key Questions Explored
1. What is the estimated size and growth trajectory of the Nigerian ride-hailing market?
2. How has Uber’s exit reshaped competitive dynamics among Bolt, inDrive, and LagRide?
3. What are the typical commission rates and driver unit economics across platforms?
4. How do pricing levels compare on key Lagos routes?
5. What are the most critical pain points for drivers and riders?
6. Where do the biggest safety and trust gaps exist?
7. What differentiation opportunities exist for a new entrant?
8. What are the key risks and intelligence gaps that require further research?

## Process
1. **Data Collection**
   - Gathered and reviewed secondary public data from industry reports, news coverage, company disclosures, and public forums.
   - Compiled pricing comparisons, commission ranges, and market size estimates.

2. **Analysis & Synthesis**
   - Compared pre- and post-Uber competitive positions.
   - Modelled illustrative driver unit economics under different fare and commission scenarios.
   - Synthesised driver and rider pain points from reviews, social commentary, and union statements.
   - Identified structural gaps and high-level go-to-market opportunities.

3. **Report Structuring**
   - Organised findings into clear sections with transparent data labelling (Sourced / Estimated / Assumption).
   - Highlighted intelligence gaps requiring primary research.

## Key Insights & Recommendations
1. **Market Opportunity**  
   Core ride-hailing revenue estimated at US$250–280 million (2024–25), with broader urban mobility closer to US$400–450 million. Positive growth outlook (≈9–11% CAGR).

2. **Post-Uber Dynamics**  
   Bolt is best positioned to absorb volume due to scale. inDrive benefits from lowest commissions and active driver recruitment. LagRide is expanding Lagos capacity with state backing.

3. **Driver Economics**  
   High commissions and rising fuel costs create thin net earnings. inDrive’s lower take-rate model is structurally more resilient in a price-sensitive market.

4. **Critical Pain Points**  
   Drivers: high commissions, low fares, safety risks, opaque penalties.  
   Riders: destination discrimination, “add small” renegotiation, vehicle quality, and safety concerns.

5. **Differentiation Opportunities**  
   - Transparent low take-rate and better net earnings visibility  
   - Elimination of destination discrimination and post-acceptance price changes  
   - Visible safety features, vehicle standards, and reliable support  
   - Flexible pricing combined with local legitimacy

6. **Strategic Direction**  
   Start dense in Lagos, prioritise driver economics and trust features, then expand selectively to other major cities once unit economics are proven.

## Conclusion
Uber’s exit has opened a meaningful window in the Nigerian ride-hailing market. While Bolt, inDrive, and LagRide are actively competing for the redistributed supply and demand, significant structural gaps remain — particularly around driver net earnings, rider trust, and safety perception.  

This report provides a clear, secondary-data-based intelligence foundation for understanding the current landscape and identifying where a well-positioned new entrant (or existing player) can create durable advantage. Primary research is recommended to validate unit economics, segmentation, and acquisition channels.
